# Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) and Lead Scoring

> The targeting model for TK's Americas business development. Used by the prospector system and for manual qualification.

## Firmographics

- **Company size:** 500 to 50,000 employees
- **Revenue:** 100M USD to 50B USD and above
- **Geography:** United States, Canada, Mexico. Existing Mexico manufacturing operations are a strong buying signal (nearshore plus offshore tooling appetite).

## Target Industries (by weight)

| Industry | Share of focus |
|---|---|
| Consumer electronics | 50 to 60 percent |
| Medical devices and diagnostics | 15 to 20 percent |
| Automotive | 15 percent |
| Telecom and appliances | 10 to 15 percent |

Packaging, cosmetics, and food and beverage are also active verticals given the depth of TK's caps and closures and thin wall portfolio.

## Technical Fit Signals

A prospect is a strong fit when they need molds that are hard to build, because that is where TK wins on capability rather than price alone:

- Multicavity tooling (32, 48, 64, 96, 128 cavity)
- Tight tolerance and high concentricity requirements
- Overmolding, 2K / two shot, insert molding
- Stack molds and high output programs
- Conformal cooling for cycle time reduction
- Thin wall parts (down to 0.25 mm)
- Unscrewing closures and complex slider actions

## Lead Scoring

| Score | Tier | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 70 plus | Hot | Prioritize, personalized outreach, route to live conversation |
| 50 to 69 | Warm | Nurture sequence, educational content |
| Below 50 | Park | Low touch, revisit if signals change |

## Buying Triggers

- Reshoring or dual sourcing tooling decisions
- A current tooling supplier missing lead times or quality
- New product launch needing high cavitation tooling fast
- Mexico operation looking to optimize landed cost
- Tariff pressure forcing a total cost rethink (see market-positioning doc)

## Disqualifiers

- Pure low complexity, low cavity commodity tooling where price is the only axis
- No tolerance for any offshore production under any structure
- Programs too small to justify high cavitation tooling investment
